In this article, it introduces a paper reading method called "THREE-PASS" approach. The _key idea_ is that you should read the paper in up to three times.

# Three-Pass Approach

## The first pass

A quick scan, you should:

1. Carefully read the title, abstract, and introduction
2. Read the section title and sub-section headings, but ignoring everything else
3. Read the conclusions
4. Glance over the references, mentally ticking off the ones you have already read

At the end of the first pass, you should be able to answer five questions:

1. Category: What type of paper is this? A measurement paper? An analysis of an existing system? A description of a research prototype?
2. Context: Which other papers is it related to? Which theoretical bases were used to analyze the problem?
3. Correctness: Do the assumptions appear to be valid?
4. Contributions: What are the paper’s main contributions?
5. Clarity: Is the paper well written?

Then you can decide whether to keep reading or not.

## The second pass

Read with greater care but ignore details such as proofs. It helps to jot down the key points, or to make comments in the margins, as you read.

1. Look carefully at the figures, diagrams and other illustrations in the paper, pay attention to whether the axes are properly labeled? Are results and conclusions statistically significant? Common mistakes like this will separate rushed, mid work from the truly excellent.
2. Remember to mark relevant unread references for further reading.

After this pass, you should understand main content of the paper, if you don't the problem might just be you are tired or the paper is poorly written or you are new to this domain so you need to learn some background material first.

## The third pass

To fully understand a paper, you may try to _virtually re-implement_ the paper, which means: making the same assumptions as the authors, re-create the work.
